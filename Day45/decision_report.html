The Real Decision

It's not really "AI vs. Full Stack" — it's how you allocate 12 months to become job-ready for AI internships. The trade-off: depth in your genuine interest (AI/DS) vs. hedging with breadth (full stack) for more roles. It's hard because you fear picking wrong costs you time you can't get back — even though you already admit you can course-correct.

The Case For Each Option

Option A — Focus on AI/DS

Strongest case: It's your degree, your genuine interest, and where you already have real depth — Circuit.IQ, RAG chatbot, DistilBERT classifier. Depth compounds; recruiters at AI-first teams filter for exactly this signal.

Hidden upside: Going deeper (LangGraph, MLOps, Docker) closes the gaps that currently separate you from mid-level AI candidates — this is what actually differentiates a fresher.

Biggest weakness: Narrower net. Fewer roles technically qualify you if pure AI/DS internships are scarce in your target window.

Best if you value: depth, career alignment, differentiation

Option B — Split time: AI + Full Stack

Strongest case: More job titles become reachable (Full Stack AI Developer, ML Engineer with product exposure) — you already have FastAPI + React, so this isn't starting from zero.

Hidden upside: Full stack skills make your AI projects more shippable and demo-able — Circuit.IQ itself only exists because you can build the frontend, not just the model.

Biggest weakness: Split focus in a 12-month window risks shallow competence in both — you may end up "good enough" nowhere, right when you need to stand out.

Best if you value: optionality, safety net, employability
Assumption Buster

  • That more breadth automatically means more opportunities — many "Full Stack AI Developer" listings actually want AI depth with basic full-stack fluency, not two full skill trees.

2 biases at play

  • Loss aversion — the fear of "wasted time" looms larger than the upside of committed depth, even though you said the plan is adjustable.
  • Status quo / safety bias — splitting effort feels safer, but safety here is an illusion since it dilutes the one edge (AI depth) that actually differentiates freshers.

1 thing you're definitely ignoring

You already have full-stack fluency good enough to ship AI products end-to-end (Circuit.IQ proves it). The real gap isn't full stack — it's AI engineering depth (Docker, LangGraph, MLOps, DSA), which your own roadmap already identifies.
