What's one thing on your profile you've been meaning to fix?
DAY 3Send 10 strategic connection requests

Who to target: AI Engineers / ML Engineers at Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and AI startups you're watching · recruiters who post about entry-level AI roles · founders of small AI startups (higher acceptance rate, more responsive) · alumni from University of Kashmir now in tech.

Hi [Name] — I'm a B.Tech AI/DS student building full-stack AI projects (RAG, LLMs). Following your work at [Company] — would love to connect and learn from your journey.
DAY 4Comment on 5 posts in your target space

The Value Comment formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.

Example: "Agree that RAG evaluation is underrated — I ran into the same issue building a document chatbot; chunking strategy mattered more than the embedding model itself. Curious what retrieval metric you found most reliable in production?"

This gets you seen by the post author (often a hiring manager) and by everyone else commenting — far higher signal than a like.

DAY 5Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take
Unpopular opinion: your first AI project shouldn't be a Kaggle notebook. It should be something that breaks in production. My RAG chatbot "worked" in testing. Then I fed it a 200-page PDF and retrieval latency tripled. Fixing that taught me more about embeddings, chunking, and vector search than any course did. Courses teach you the theory. Deployment teaches you where the theory lies to you. If you're learning AI right now — ship something small, put it in front of real inputs, and let it break. That's the fastest way I've found to actually learn this stuff. What's something you learned only after your project broke?
DAY 6Engage + send 5 more connections

Reply to every comment on Posts #1 and #2 — even a one-line reply keeps the post alive in others' feeds via LinkedIn's algorithm. Send 5 more connection requests using the Day 3 template, targeting people who engaged with your posts.

DAY 7Review your numbers

Profile views: If they've roughly doubled from your pre-rebuild baseline, your headline/keywords are working. If flat, your headline still isn't keyword-matched to what recruiters search — revisit Option 1.

Connection acceptance rate: Above 40% is healthy for cold outreach to strangers in tech. Below that, your connection message is too generic — personalize the [Company]/[Name] fields more specifically next time.

Post engagement: Comments matter more than likes. If Post #2 got more comments than Post #1, lean into hot-takes/lessons-learned as your content format going forward.
